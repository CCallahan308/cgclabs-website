The problem

The marketplace owns the customer. That is the whole problem.

A listing is a shelf you rent. The platform sets the fees, changes the rules, and keeps the buyer data. You do the work; it keeps the relationship.

  • Fees on every order, whether it profits or not
  • Fee and policy changes announced, not negotiated
  • A customer list you cannot see, let alone contact
  • Your brand living under someone else's logo
What I build

A store you own, built to sell.

01

Storefront design and build

Product pages, collections, cart, and checkout on a stack your team can run without a developer. Built to load fast on the phones your customers actually use.

02

Catalog migration

Listings exported from Etsy and eBay, cleaned and re-structured: titles, pricing, variants, and photos brought into one catalog with a single source of truth.

03

Payments and operations

Checkout, tax, and shipping rules configured, order notifications wired to wherever you already work. The store runs; you watch it.

04

The selling report

The practice is analytics first, so the store comes with a plain one-page report: which products move and which listings stall, checked once a week.

05

Handover

Domain, code, and customer data in your name, with a runbook short enough to actually read.

Ownership

At handoff, you own all of it.

No seat licenses. No monthly fee to me. No platform standing between you and your customers.

  • Your domain
  • Your storefront and its code
  • Your product data
  • Your customer list
Engagement

Fixed scope. Priced in writing. Built in weeks.

A storefront is a project, not a subscription. Scope is defined before work starts: catalog, features, design, one written price. The build is sequenced so the shop keeps selling on the marketplace until the new store is ready to take over.
