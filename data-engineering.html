The problem

The data exists. Getting it ready is the work.

Years of records sit across databases, exports, and flat files. Before your team can analyze them or build a model, someone has to reconcile the formats, check the records, and make delivery repeatable.

  • Records scattered across formats, systems, and years
  • No shared schema, so the same field means three things
  • Missing values, duplicate records, and totals that do not reconcile
  • Manual exports with no reliable refresh or failure alert
What I do

From source system to usable dataset.

01

Extraction

Historical records pulled out of legacy systems, exports, and paper-era flat files, with the source system left intact.

02

Cleaning and normalization

Deduplication, schema mapping, and reconciliation of conflicting definitions, so one record means one thing everywhere.

03

Data quality checks

Checks for missing fields, duplicate records, row counts, and reconciled totals. Invalid records follow an agreed handling policy rather than passing through silently.

04

Analytics and training datasets

Shared field definitions and a schema built around the intended use. When training labels are needed, the classification rules are part of the handoff.

05

Scheduled pipelines

Load into your warehouse or project environment, with a refresh schedule, failure alerts, and instructions for safe reruns agreed in the scope.

06

Lineage and handoff

Code, source-to-field mappings, quality rules, and a runbook. Your team can trace a record and understand how to operate the pipeline.

Engagement

Scoping starts with the source.

An engagement begins with an inventory: what data exists, where it lives, and what condition it is in. We agree on the destination, field definitions, quality checks, refresh schedule, and handoff. You get a fixed scope and a written price before the build.

For the first conversation, bring a list of source systems, the output you need, and the manual step or failure slowing your team down. Access requirements are agreed before any data moves.
